There are excellent ones from serious developers, and they all share one operating principle - your audio goes in and comes back damaged in a specific, characterful way. What "lo-fi" actually means in a plugin

Nearly everything sold as lo-fi is a form of subtraction or contamination. The main families:

  • Bit reduction - fewer amplitude steps, so quantisation error rides along as crackle and, deep enough, a gated rasp.
  • Sample-rate reduction / decimation - fewer time steps, so anything above half the new rate folds back as inharmonic aliasing. That is the metallic sheen.
  • Tape and cassette emulation - saturation, head bump, hiss, and pitch instability in the form of wow and flutter.
  • Vinyl - crackle, surface noise, rumble, and the band-limiting of an old cutting chain.
  • Band-limiting and speaker sims - the radio, telephone and small-speaker tones that come from throwing away the extremes.

Every one of those is applied to the source. Mute the plugin and your recording is intact underneath, because the recording was always what was making the sound. That is the definition of an effect, and it is not a criticism - most of the time it is exactly right.

What TALLBOY does instead

TALLBOY listens to a monophonic track, works out what pitch you are singing or playing and how the note is shaped, and then discards the audio entirely. The pitch and envelope drive four modelled sound-chip channels:

  • Two pulse channels, each with four selectable duty cycles - the thin nasal one, the hollow one, the fat square.
  • A 32-sample, 4-bit wavetable for the softer, rounder chip timbre.
  • An LFSR noise channel keyed off transients, so consonants and pick attacks land as hardware noise rather than mush.

Nothing of your waveform survives. That is the point. Crush a vocal and you get a crunchy vocal; resynthesise it and you get a chip voice singing your hook, bending when you bend, cutting off when you stop. That is what people mean when they say "8-bit," and no amount of degradation gets there, because degradation cannot invent a pulse wave.

Quick test for which tool you need: if you would still describe the result as "my recording, but ___", you want a lo-fi plugin. If you want to describe it as "a chip playing my part", you want resynthesis.

When a lo-fi plugin is the better tool

  • You want your performance, aged. The timbre, the room, the human detail - kept, but pushed through a degraded path. Resynthesis destroys all of that by design.
  • Your source is polyphonic or percussive. Drums, chords, loops, full mixes. Pitch tracking has nothing to lock onto, so TALLBOY's headline feature simply does not apply.
  • You want tape, vinyl or cassette specifically. Wow, flutter, crackle, hiss and head bump are not in TALLBOY at all. A dedicated emulation will beat it every time because TALLBOY does not compete there.
  • You want deep control over the degradation itself. A plugin whose entire interface is one process will usually offer more shaping of that process than one third of a three-cartridge plugin driven by a D-pad.
  • You are doing bus or master processing. Lo-fi glue across a whole mix is a degradation job, full stop.

When TALLBOY is the right call

  • You want a chip voice, not chip texture. A pulse wave performing your melody, not your vocal with grit on it.
  • Your source is monophonic. Vocals, vocal doubles, bass, single-note leads and hooks - one clear pitch, one clear envelope.
  • You want an 8-bit double. Blend the resynthesised chip line under the real take and the vocal grows a companion locked to every bend, slide and cut-off.
  • You want the part written for you. Held note in, tempo-synced arcade sequence out, four octaves of it.
  • You want a decimator too. CRUSH ROM is in the box, so the $20 covers both the resynthesiser and a bit/rate crusher with jitter and a post filter.
  • You want simple licensing. One-time $20, no subscription, no iLok, three machines, free demo on macOS and Windows.

The bottom line

The lo-fi category is not TALLBOY's competition so much as its neighbour, and a lot of records want both: a tape or crush plugin holding the drums and the bus together, and a chip voice doubling the hook. Ask what you want the output to be. If the answer is "this recording, but rougher, older, smaller," reach for a lo-fi plugin - they are good at it and TALLBOY does not try to take that job. If the answer is "a sound chip playing this part," no degradation plugin at any price will get you there, because you cannot subtract your way to a pulse wave.
