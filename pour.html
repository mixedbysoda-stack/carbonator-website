FAQ

Frequently asked.

What is a stereo imager?
A stereo imager shapes the width and placement of your stereo field. It separates the middle (mono) content from the side (stereo) content so you can widen, narrow, tilt, or rebalance without re-recording. Pour gives you all the classic M/S controls plus a live polar vectorscope.
What is Mid/Side processing?
Mid/Side encodes L and R into Middle (L+R, the mono part) and Side (L-R, the stereo-only part). Most of Pour's controls operate in this domain so you can affect only the stereo content without touching the mono center. The plugin decodes back to L/R on output.
What do Shuffle and Space do?
Space applies a broad bass-frequency boost or cut to the side signal. Shuffle sets the crossover point for that shaping (40 Hz to 400 Hz). Together they implement Alan Blumlein's 1931 "Stereo Shuffling" — the same trick SSL's Fusion hardware uses.
What's the difference between Width, Rotation, and Asymmetry?
Width scales the side signal (±6 dB) to widen or narrow the image. Rotation tilts the entire stereo field left or right by rotating the M/S vector. Asymmetry shifts the phantom center to one side without affecting width. The live vectorscope overlay shows all three at once.
Will Pour make my mix mono-incompatible?
Default settings are unity. Moderate Blumlein Stereo Shuffling is safe for mono summation. Push Width, Space, and Rotation hard and you can cause phase cancellation on fold-down. Hit the Solo Side button to preview exactly what drops out when the signal collapses to mono.
What formats are supported?
