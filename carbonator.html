5 DSP flavors

Pick your flavor.

Each flavor is a carefully engineered DSP chain. One knob — Fizz — morphs all parameters simultaneously from subtle to savage.

01 / Cola

Analog console warmth.

Console-style saturation that glues your mix with even harmonics, subtle compression, and the warmth of classic analog hardware.

Signal chain: Pre-Gain (1x–6x) → Asymmetric Soft Clip → DC Blocker → Compressor (1.5:1–8:1) → Tilt EQ → Auto Makeup Gain
Flat mode: Tape saturation layer + 60Hz hum

Drums · Full Mix · Bass · Guitars

02 / Cherry

Aggressive tube distortion.

Tube-style distortion with odd harmonics and aggressive clipping. From subtle grit to full-on fuzz, Cherry delivers raw energy.

Signal chain: Pre-Gain (1x–10x) → Tube Waveshaper → DC Blocker → Presence EQ (+6dB @ 3kHz) → Output Limiter → Auto Makeup Gain
Flat mode: Transistor clipping + high shelf cut

Vocals · Synths · Drums · Parallel

03 / Grape

Bitcrusher & lo-fi.

Digital destruction with bit reduction and sample rate decimation. Grape crushes your signal into crunchy, retro lo-fi textures.

Signal chain: Pre-Gain → Bit Crusher (16-bit → 4-bit) → Sample Rate Decimate (44.1kHz → 4kHz) → Jitter → LP Filter → Auto Makeup Gain
Flat mode: Vinyl noise + wow & flutter

Lo-Fi · Drums · Synths · Creative

04 / Lemon-Lime

Bright harmonic exciter.

Harmonic exciter that adds sparkle and air without harshness. Lemon-Lime enhances presence and clarity with musical high-frequency saturation.

Signal chain: HP Filter (above 3kHz) → Harmonic Generator (2nd + 3rd) → Air Shelf (+6dB @ 12kHz) → Dynamic Saturation → Mix Blend → Auto Makeup Gain
Flat mode: Subtle stereo widening + de-essing

Vocals · Acoustic · Mix Bus · Cymbals

05 / Orange Cream

Resonant filter + drive.

Resonant lowpass filter with warm drive. Orange Cream sweeps from open and clean to thick, squelchy filter tones with musical resonance.

Signal chain: Pre-Drive → Resonant LP Filter (20kHz → 200Hz) → Resonance (Q 0.7–4.0) → Low Shelf Boost → Soft Saturator → Auto Makeup Gain
Flat mode: Dirtier — tanh drive, LP to 100Hz, more resonance

Synths · Bass · Pads · Creative

Use cases

When to use Carbonator.

Each flavor is tuned for specific instruments and production styles. Here's what producers reach for most.

Drums & Percussion

Cola flavor — Fizz 60–80%

Console-style warmth that glues kick and snare together. Adds punch and cohesion without muddying low frequencies. The built-in compressor stage keeps transients intact.

Vocals

Grape flavor — Fizz 30–50%

Lo-fi character with bit reduction and digital artifacts. Sits vocals forward in the mix with even-order harmonics. Go above 70% for aggressive creative effects.

Bass & Synths

Cherry flavor — Fizz 40–70%

Aggressive tube-style distortion with odd harmonics. Adds grit and presence to sub-heavy bass lines and analog synth patches.

Hi-Hats & Acoustic Guitar

Lemon-Lime flavor — Fizz 20–50%

Harmonic exciter that adds air and sparkle to high-frequency content. Makes cymbals shimmer and acoustic guitars shine without harshness.

Pads & Keys

Orange Cream flavor — Fizz 30–60%

Warm resonant lowpass filter with smooth drive. Beautiful on sustained sounds, adding movement and analog character to digital patches.

Full Mix Bus

Carbonated mode — Fizz 20–40%

Blends all 5 saturation circuits for transparent console glue. Use at low Fizz settings for subtle cohesion across the entire mix.

How it works
